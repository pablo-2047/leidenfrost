Hydrodynamic Spreading, Solutal Marangoni Effects & Leidenfrost Rebound Dynamics of Binary Droplets

A systematic, high-speed shadowgraphy investigation (4,000 fps) quantifying the post-impact morphology of binary droplets impinging upon superheated polished aluminum substrates across 10 liquid systems: pure deionized water and binary mixtures of water with acetone (20%, 40%, 60% v/v), ethanol (20%, 40%, 60% v/v), and methanol (25%, 40%, 60% v/v). Across 936 discrete trials spanning temperatures from 100°C to 400°C and drop heights from 1.3 cm to 15.6 cm, we evaluate apex versus 3D volume center-of-mass height trajectories, dynamic contact diameters, explosive transition fragmentation, and the universal Clanet scaling law βmax ≈ 1.34 · We1/4.

📐 Mathematical Framework & Non-Dimensional Normalization ▼ Expand Theory & Equations

All raw temporal and spatial coordinates extracted from the 4,000 fps video series are non-dimensionalized using classical capillary-inertial scaling to enable universal cross-fluid comparison:

Impact Velocity & Time Scale
V = √(2gh), τ = D₀ / V
Impact velocity V from release height h; capillary-inertial convective time scale τ.
Non-Dimensional Time
t* = (t - t_contact) / τ
Where t_contact is the exact frame immediately preceding contact (4,000 fps resolution).
Spreading & Contact Factors
β(t) = D_spread(t) / D₀, β_c = D_contact / D₀
Normalized by pre-impact equivalent spherical droplet diameter D₀.
3D Volume Center of Mass Height
H_cm(t) / D₀ (Axisymmetric Disk Integration)
True volume center of mass z_cm = π∑y·r²Δy / π∑r²Δy, eliminating satellite jet artifacts.
Weber & Reynolds Numbers
We = ρV²D₀ / σ, Re = ρVD₀ / μ
Inertia to surface tension ratio (We) and viscous dissipation ratio (Re).
Universal Leidenfrost Scaling
β_max ≈ 1.34 · We^(1/4) (Clanet / Biance)
Capillary-inertial balance on friction-free vapor cushion, verified across all 10 fluids.
